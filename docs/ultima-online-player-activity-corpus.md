# Ultima Online player activity corpus

A reference on what Ultima Online players did, from public launch through 2026.

---

## How to read this document

1. Ultima Online is a persistent simulated world, not a quest-driven theme park.
2. Every activity belongs to an era and a ruleset. The same verb (kill, steal, build, sell) means different things in 1997 Felucca, 2003 Age of Shadows Trammel, 2010 High Seas, 2018 Endless Journey, 2024 New Legacy, or a 2026 T2A free shard.
3. Official Origin, EA, Mythic, and Broadsword shards are different from emulator "free shards." Both are part of player practice.
4. There are no classes, no character levels, no final boss, and no win condition. No endgame retires a character.
5. Whether players PvP depends on the facet and the shard. It is not true that everyone PvPs, and it is not true that no one does.
6. Player activity is concrete: mine, smith, mark a rune, refresh a house, flag criminal, loot a corpse, place a vendor, donate to a city collection.

The official public launch was 24 September 1997, not 1998.

## 0. The world

### Setting

Sosaria / Britannia and later continents, drawn from the single-player Ultima series. Lord British is the in-fiction monarch and also Richard Garriott's avatar. Cities include Britain, Trinsic, Yew, Minoc, Vesper, Moonglow, Magincia, Jhelom, Skara Brae, Serpent's Hold, Buccaneer's Den, plus later cities on new facets.

### Server model

Each live copy of the world is a shard. Official shards have names (Atlantic, Pacific, Great Lakes, Lake Superior, Chesapeake, Catskills, Baja, Sonoma, Europa, Drachenfels, Oceania, Japanese shards such as Asuka, Yamato, Wakoku, Izumo, Sakura, and others). A character does not automatically exist on every shard. In later years, characters could transfer between official shards under restrictions.

### Clients

- Classic 2D client (original, still used)
- Third Dawn 3D client (2001)
- Enhanced Client (later)
- In 2026, official work continues on Classic Client GPU rendering

Players also use third-party assistive tools. Official policy treats many of them as cheating.

### Account tiers (modern official)

- Paid subscription (veteran)
- Endless Journey free-to-play (2018 on)
- New Legacy: a separate seasonal official shard with its own wipe cycle

### Core design

Skills rise by use. The world persists while the player is offline. Almost any object can be picked up, used, dyed, locked down, stolen, or used as a physical obstacle. The designers meant ecology, economy, and reputation to be simulated. Players immediately exploited and reinvented all three.

## 1. Character model

A character is not a class. It has these parts:

| Part | Details |
|---|---|
| Appearance | Human from launch; elves with Mondain's Legacy (2005); gargoyles with Stygian Abyss (2009). Gender, skin, hair, and clothing are cosmetic and dyeable. |
| Three statistics | Strength, Dexterity, Intelligence. Early individual caps near 100; later 125 real with item bonuses and a combined cap around 150 including bonuses. Health, stamina, and mana derive from stats. |
| Skill points | Historical total cap 700, later 720 on official shards. Individual skills cap at 100.0 and Power Scrolls raise them to 120.0. Skills can be locked, set to decrease, or transferred through Soulstones on later official rulesets. |
| Titles | Skill titles (Neophyte through Legendary) plus fame/karma reputation titles, champion titles, virtue titles, guild titles, and event titles. |
| Status flags | Notoriety / karma / fame, murder counts, criminal flags, and (after Age of Shadows) item insurance state. |

The starting kit historically included clothing, a dagger or practice weapon, a book, a candle, and gold. Players choose a few starting skills. NPCs train many skills cheaply to a low ceiling (often around 30-40).

### How players raise characters

Use the skill. Fight with a sword to raise Swordsmanship. Cast spells to raise Magery. Chop trees to raise Lumberjacking. Or pay an NPC.

Later methods: New Player Quest accelerated gain, Power Hour (Renaissance era), Scrolls of Alacrity and Transcendence, the New Haven accelerated zone, and New Legacy accelerated seasonal gain.

### Common templates

These are player builds, not official classes:

- Pure mage
- Warrior / sampire (sword + Chivalry + Necromancy)
- Tamer / mage
- Bard
- Thief
- Crafter with combat skills
- Archer / ranger
- Ninja
- Paladin
- Hybrid "jack of all trades" that is weak at everything

On classic shards, seven skills at 100 was the standard 7xGM template.

The official live game has about 58 skills plus sub-skills (Glassblowing under Alchemy, Masonry under Carpentry, Basket Weaving under Tinkering). Discordance replaced Enticement.

| Archery | Hit chance with bows / crossbows; special moves after AoS |
| Wrestling | Unarmed combat |
| Throwing | Gargoyle-era thrown weapons (Stygian Abyss, 2009) |
| Tactics | Melee / ranged damage |
| Anatomy | Damage and bandage heal amount |

| Skill | What players use it for |

The classic Magery reagents are Black Pearl, Blood Moss, Garlic, Ginseng, Mandrake Root, Nightshade, Spider's Silk, and Sulfurous Ash.

|---|---|

Dragons, nightmares, later rare hues, and expansion creatures are high-status pets. Stables hold pets while the owner is offline. Stable slot counts increased over time.

### Bardic

Musicianship is the parent skill.

| Skill | What players use it for |
|---|---|
| Peacemaking | Stop combat |
| Provocation | Force creatures (or, in some eras and rules, players) to fight each other |
| Discordance | Debuff |

Bards are a complete playstyle: dungeon control, champion-spawn support, PvP disruption.

### Crafting

| Skill | What players use it for |
|---|---|
| Blacksmithy | Metal weapons, armor, tools |
| Tailoring | Cloth and leather armor, clothing, decorations |
| Carpentry | Furniture, wooden weapons, house add-ons |
| Masonry | Stone furniture / house pieces (Carpentry sub-skill) |
| Tinkering | Tools, jewelry, traps, golem parts, lockpicks |
| Basket Weaving | Tinkering sub-skill |
| Cooking | Food |
| Bowcraft / Fletching | Bows, arrows, related wooden weapons |

Crafters make weapons, armor, clothing, furniture, house add-ons, tools, jewelry, traps, golem parts, lockpicks, deedable decorations, food, maps, runebooks, and repair deeds. Quality tiers and exceptional crafts matter. Runic tools and Bulk Order Deeds (BODs) dominate later crafter loops.

### Skill Masteries (Time of Legends, 2015)

Selected high-skill characters unlock mastery abilities in a mastery spellbook, with intensity tiers. This is late-era official content. It was not part of 1997-2004 play.

## 3. Geography

### Original Britannia (1997)

Towns, wilderness, dungeons (Destard, Covetous, Despise, Shame, Hythloth, Deceit, Wrong, Fire, Ice, and others), shrines of the Virtues, moongates.

### Lost Lands / The Second Age (1998)

Delucia, Papua, new dungeons and jungle.

### Facets after Renaissance (2000)

Felucca is the original ruleset world: non-consensual PvP, stealing, and corpse loot. Champion-spawn rewards are often better there, and Factions historically lived there.

Trammel has near-duplicate geography, but unprovoked PvP and theft are forbidden. Most of the population moved to Trammel after 2000.

### Later landmasses

| Expansion | Year | Land / content |
|---|---|---|
| Third Dawn | 2001 | Ilshenar; 3D client option |
| Age of Shadows | 2003 | Malas, including Luna and Umbra |
| Samurai Empire | 2004 | Tokuno Islands |
| Stygian Abyss | 2009 | Ter Mur and the Stygian Abyss dungeon |
| High Seas | 2010 | Ocean, pirates, floating village (not a full continent) |
| Time of Legends | 2015 | Valley of Eodon and Shadowguard |

### Travel methods

- Walk / run (stamina)
- Mounts: horses from launch; later llamas, ostards, nightmares, fire steeds, and many expansion mounts
- Boats: place a deeded ship and command it by speech ("forward," "left," "drop anchor"). High Seas adds galleons, cannon, hold cargo, and ship-to-ship combat
- Public moongates between towns
- Magery Mark / Recall / Gate Travel. Players maintain runes and runebooks (and later Runic Atlases). Public "rune libraries" in player houses are a major social institution
- Moonstones in very early design; later mostly obsolete
- Recall / gate into houses if the owner allows public access, or through runes marked at the house
- New Legacy restricts travel more toward walking, town gates, ferries, and limited mark points

## 4. Combat, death, flags, and loot

### Combat loop

Toggle war mode. Double-click a target or use an attack macro. Swing speed depends on weapon and stamina / Dexterity. Mages interrupt, field-spell, and explode at range. Bandages take time. Pets and summons add extra attackers.

After Age of Shadows, weapons have primary and secondary special moves (Armor Ignore, Double Strike, Whirlwind, Move, Bleed, and many others) that cost mana.

### Death

The character becomes a ghost, can speak in italic ghost-speech, and must find a healer NPC, a player with Resurrection, or a shrine / ankh. In early UO, death was frequent and punishing.

### Loot and insurance

From 1997 through the early years, looting was full. Anyone could take gear from a corpse. House keys on a corpse meant the house could be emptied. Players fought in cheap "suit" gear and banked their valuables.

Age of Shadows (2003) added Item Insurance. A player pays gold so an item returns to the backpack on resurrection. Uninsured items still drop. Insurance is the mechanical reason Trammel PvM became item-property hunting rather than corpse recovery.

- Some items cannot be insured (cursed items, many stackables).
- In PvP, the killer can receive a portion of the victim's insurance gold.

### Reputation flags

Names vary by era:

- Innocent (blue)
- Criminal (gray)
- Murderer (red)
- Council / guild hues

Attacking an innocent in a guarded town summons NPC guards who kill the attacker.

Murder counts accumulate. Enough unpardoned murders turn the character red. Reds are attackable, often cannot use certain vendors or towns safely, and on some rulesets suffer stat loss on death.

Early systems: notoriety, bounties (exploited by suicide-for-split), Order versus Chaos guilds.

Players specialized as PKs, anti-PKs, bounty hunters, and "sheep" (victims). This social ecology mattered as much as the monster ecology.

### PvP forms

- Open-world ganks on Felucca and classic shards
- Guild wars
- Faction warfare (True Britannians, Council of Mages, Minax, Shadowlords) over town control on Felucca
- Later, Vice versus Virtue
- Consensual duels and pits
- Siege Perilous / Siege-rules shards: harsher, often no Trammel, no item insurance, slower gains
- House-block sieges using furniture in 1997-98
- Gate-camping banks
- Boat PvP after High Seas

## 5. Economy

### Gathering

- Mine mountains and cave floors with pickaxe or shovel (iron through colored ore tiers in later eras; granite for masonry)
- Chop trees
- Fish (basic fish; later High Seas deep-sea, crabs, lobster, messages in bottles, sea serpents)
- Hunt for hides, meat, feathers, reagents, scales
- Harvest sand for glass
- Farm champion and peerless reagents

### Crafting and Bulk Order Deeds

Crafters fill Bulk Order Deeds from NPC crafters for gold and reward points (runic kits, recipes, ancient hammers). After the BOD system matured, it became a primary gold printer for dedicated crafters on official shards.

### Player vendors

Place a vendor in a house and stock it with priced items. The vendor sells while the owner is offline and pays daily upkeep from its own gold. Rental vendors let other players rent shop space. Entire streets of vendor houses form unofficial malls (Britain, Luna after Malas, and shard-specific hubs).

### Banking

Say "bank" near a banker. The bank box is account / character storage. Checks, commodity deeds, and later account gold reduced gold-bar logistics. In the early game, players hauled thousands of gold piles and were robbed at the bank steps.

### Markets

NPC buy / sell tables set floors. Player prices float.

Rare items include:

- Unusual dyed clothing
- Named / artifact weapons
- Power Scrolls (105-120)
- Scrolls of Transcendence
- Rare pet hues
- House add-ons
- Decorative rares from events
- Stealable museum-style objects
- 1-of-1 New Legacy heirlooms

### Gold sinks and sources

Sinks: insurance, vendor upkeep, house placement / conversion, tithing for Chivalry, reagents, repairs, NPC training, imbuing ingredients.

Sources: loot, BODs, player sales, champion scrolls sold to others, gathering.

### Real-money trade

Real-money trade (RMT) has persisted since the late 1990s. It is officially against the Terms of Service. Free shards often sell gold or items as a server business model. RMT was never an intended gameplay pillar, but players did it.

## 6. Housing

Housing is a second game inside the game.

### What housing is for

- Storage (locked-down items and secure containers)
- Vendor malls
- Guild halls
- Decoration showpieces
- Taverns, libraries, museums
- Role-play venues
- Rune libraries
- Plot ownership

### Placement

Use a House Placement Tool on valid terrain. Modern official rules allow one primary house per account. Earlier eras were wilder (multiple houses, then condemned-house rules). Co-owner and friends lists control access. The public or private house setting matters for visitors and marked runes.

### Customization

Houses came as prefabricated deeds at first. Age of Shadows introduced custom housing (foundation, walls, floors, stairs). Samurai Empire added roofs. Later tile sets include Gothic, Rustic, King's Collection, Time of Legends stonework, and anniversary furniture. Players treat decoration as a hobby equal to raiding.

### Decay and IDOC

Houses refresh when the owner uses them (and, under primary-house rules, when the account stays active).

Decay stages:

1. Like New
2. Slightly Worn
3. Somewhat Worn
4. Fairly Worn
5. Greatly Worn
6. In Danger of Collapsing (IDOC)

IDOC houses attract crowds. When the house falls, its contents spill. Later official rules spawn Grubbers that run off with loot, barrel-fence the lot, and delete some item types. Camping IDOCs is a recognized player occupation.

### Housing as warfare and grief

Early players blocked doors and streets with furniture. In one documented carpenter action, players walled off parts of Trinsic. House keys looted from corpses allowed burglary of the entire building. That is why keys, secure containers, and later access lists matter.

## 7. Structured PvE content

Official development added these repeatable loops on top of the sandbox.

| Content | What players do |
|---|---|
| Dungeons and world spawns | Always present. |
| Treasure maps | Decipher (Cartography), find the chest, pick or explode it, fight guardians. |
| Champion spawns | Altar, four waves, then a named Champion (examples: Rikktor, Semidar, Mephitis, Neira, Lord Oaks, Barracoon, and later additions). Felucca champs drop Power Scrolls and skulls. The Valor virtue can advance an altar. Titles accrue for repeat participation. The Harrower is a meta-champion assembled from skulls. |
| Peerless bosses | Instanced or gated bosses (example: Lady Melisande; Doom artifacts after AoS). Players farm them repeatedly for rare drops. |
| Quests | Sparse in 1997; substantial after Lord Blackthorn's Revenge and especially from Mondain's Legacy on, plus New Player Quests. |
| Virtues | Eight virtues with mechanical rewards: Compassion, Honesty, Honor, Humility, Justice, Sacrifice, Spirituality, Valor. Examples: Honesty discounts and lost-item returns; Sacrifice self-rez; Valor champion control; Justice anti-red bonuses. The set was completed in Time of Legends. |
| City loyalty and elections | Rank with a city, become governor on veteran accounts, unlock city benefits. |
| Community collections | Donate items to Britannia Library, Vesper Museum, Moonglow Zoo; Cleanup Britannia. |
| Void Pool | Void Pool and similar repeatable PvM. |
| Rotating events | Events in the style of Treasures of Tokuno. |
| Seasonal events | Halloween (trick-or-treat, skeletons, jack-o'-lanterns), Christmas / Krampus, anniversary gifts, 2026 Shardfall (donate combustibles for city fireworks; fight Echo of Mondain), Huntmaster's Challenge, weekend shard events. |
| High Seas loop | Fish, fight pirates, run cargo, cannon combat, dock at the floating village. |
| Imbuing / artifact refinement | Endgame gear crafting after Stygian Abyss. |

## 8. Magic, religion, and build identity after 2003

Before Age of Shadows, a character's identity was its skill mix plus dyed clothes plus house. After Age of Shadows, identity also includes item properties (Hit Chance Increase, Defense Chance Increase, Swing Speed, Spell Damage, Resists, skill bonuses, Mage Weapon, and others).

Spellbooks players maintain:

- Magery spellbook (circles 1-8)
- Book of Necromancy
- Book of Chivalry (tithing)
- Book of Bushido / Ninjitsu
- Spellweaving book
- Mysticism book
- Mastery spellbook (Time of Legends)

Reagents, tithing points, and focus items are resource constraints. They create supply work for gatherers and alchemists.

## 9. Social systems players invented or were given

### Guilds

Names, abbreviations, wars, alliances, guildstones, and later guild leveling on New Legacy. Guilds are the primary raid, PvP, and housing unit.

### Role-play

Taverns, courts, churches of the Virtues, evil cults, town watches, theater troupes, weddings, funerals for real-world deaths, coordinated outfits, player newspapers and books.

### Crime and scams players ran

- Bank-step murder and loot
- Gate to a remote island or dungeon after the victim withdraws gold
- Booby-trapped chests
- Fake "free gold" piles that are trapped or bait
- Vendor price fraud
- Stealing from open backpacks in crowded banks
- Red-on-red bounty fraud in the early bounty system

### Civic play

Player towns, unofficial mayors, escorts of miners, paid gate service (mages opening gates as a taxi), tour guides, paid body-escorts through PK land.

### Information play

Mapping moongate destinations in 1997. Maintaining rune libraries. Publishing vendor locations. Calling IDOCs. Calling champion altars.

### Events

Developer / Event Moderator story arcs (Blackthorn, Exodus, Minax). Player-run events. Fan fests in Austin and elsewhere from 2000 on.

### Emergent occupations the code did not name

Landlord, mall tycoon, rune librarian, IDOC hunter, power-scroll farmer, BOD factory, boat pirate, anti-PK sheriff, deco artist, book author.

## 11. What players do in 2026

### Official production shards

PvM in Trammel and Abyss-era dungeons; Felucca champs and PvP on high-population shards such as Atlantic; BOD crafting; vendor malls; house deco; seasonal events (Shardfall, Halloween, anniversary); Huntmaster; peerless updates; New Legacy Season 2 preparation; the choice between the Classic and Enhanced clients.

### Endless Journey

Explore, quest, gather basic resources, join champs, use virtues except Justice, donate to collections, do limited BODs. No house placement and no full political play.

### New Legacy

Follow the jobs board and narrative path, pick a skill set in Ocllo, gain skills by quest rather than only by raw use, join a leveling guild, hunt seasonal uniques.

### Free shards

The era comes first.

- T2A shards: open PvP, no item properties, corpse loot, raw resource PvP
- UOR shards: Trammel comfort plus Felucca risk
- AoS+ shards: itemization and custom housing
- Custom shards add markets, extra skills, PvE-only rules, or player towns with extra tools

Population is a fraction of the 2003 peak (about 250,000 subscribers). The game is still live, with a lasting core of players and a large emulator scene. Broadsword does not publish current subscriber counts, so no subscriber count after the mid-2000s is an official fact.

## 12. Master list of player actions

This is the inventory of what players do. Not every action is true in every era; section 10 gives the rulesets.

### Survival and movement

Create character; train at dummy; buy from NPC; bank; eat; drink potions; resurrect; recall; gate; boat command; mount; hide; stealth; camp; track.

### Violence

Hunt animals; clear dungeon spawns; tank with pets; bard-provoke rooms; kill other players; loot corpses; chop bodies in early years; guard-bait; accept or decline duel; siege a house entrance; fire ship cannon.

### Production

Mine; chop; fish; smelt; smith; tailor; carpenter; tinker; cook; scribe; fletch; alchemize; imbue; fill BODs; repair; craft house add-ons; craft boats.

### Trade

Stock a vendor; shop player malls; hawk goods in Britain bank; write books and sell them; flip rares; run gold to the bank under escort.

### Property

Place house; customize; lock down deco; set secure; add co-owner; place vendors; refresh house; watch IDOC; loot collapse; lose house to account inactivity.

### Animals

Tame; name; bond; stable; vet-heal; breed or hue-hunt on shards that allow it; show pets.

### Crime

Steal; snoop; poison a blade; trap a chest; pick a dungeon lock; pick a treasure chest; beg.

### Civic and meta

Join guild; declare war; join faction; vote city loyalty; donate museum fish; complete virtue paths; attend EM event; write in-game books; marry; hold funeral; run a tavern night; maintain a rune library.

### Collection

Power Scrolls; artifacts; event hues; stealables; holiday decorative tiles; museum donations; New Legacy family heirlooms.

### Out of game but part of the culture

Free-shard hopping; UO fansites and Discord; real-world fan fests; arguing Trammel versus Felucca; arguing AoS itemization versus classic; RMT; botting and anti-cheat fights.
